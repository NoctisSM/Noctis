About

Product designer building systems for high-stakes environments.

I design complex sociotechnical systems where decisions matter: police dispatch operations, healthcare platforms serving millions, and enterprise tools that must operate under pressure. My approach combines rigorous qualitative research with functional prototyping to solve problems where design directly impacts safety, efficiency, and human decision-making.

I started in graphic design, learning visual systems thinking and clear communication. Transitioning to UX, I discovered that the most compelling problems involve understanding complex workflows and designing systems people actually need. My HCI training at Georgia Tech formalized this through semi-structured interviews, contextual inquiry, affinity mapping, and journey mapping.

I have worked with Dahlstrom Technologies and Align Technology, designing products and systems that balance precision with user empathy. At Align, I redesigned the patient dashboard for 12 million users worldwide and contributed to e-commerce experiences that simplified complex healthcare decisions.

How I Work

Most of my work is 0→1: there is no existing product to improve, only a problem space nobody has structured yet. That changes the job. You cannot A/B test your way to a first version, so the research has to be good enough to commit to an architecture before anything exists.

01

Find the real constraint

Semi-structured interviews and contextual inquiry in the actual environment — a dispatch floor, a homeowner's decision, a hiring coordinator's inbox. I am looking for the constraint people have quietly organized their whole workflow around, because that is what the product has to absorb.

02

Commit to a structure

Affinity mapping and journey mapping until the problem has a shape, then an information architecture I can defend out loud. On a 0→1 project the architecture is the product decision; everything downstream inherits it.

03

Prototype something functional

I build prototypes real enough to be wrong in useful ways — working interactions, real content, real edge cases. I design in code when that is the fastest way to learn, which also means handoff is a conversation rather than a document.

04

Ship it and keep the receipts

Launch, then watch what people actually do. I am explicit about which of my decisions are validated and which are still bets — on the case studies here, reconstructions are labeled as reconstructions and unmeasured outcomes are not dressed up as metrics.
