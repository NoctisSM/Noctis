Overview

A considered purchase needs clarity before conversion

Solar pool heating sits between home improvement and a technical service. A homeowner may know the outcome they want, a warmer pool without recurring gas costs, but not how rooftop thermal panels work, how much collector area they need, or whether their existing system should be repaired or replaced.

The website needed to support several levels of intent at once: first-time buyers comparing heating options, existing owners with a leak or no-heat problem, and homeowners preparing for roof work or a property purchase.

"How might the experience make a specialized system feel understandable, credible, and easy to act on?"

Problem

One website had to serve new buyers and existing system owners

The service model is broader than installation. The company installs new systems, repairs leaks and no-heat issues, handles warranty replacements, removes and reinstalls panels for reroofing, and inspects aging or inherited systems.

A single generic contact path would have hidden those differences. The design challenge was to create a clear front door while preserving direct routes for people who already knew what was wrong.

"The organizing principle became customer situation, not product terminology."

This case study documents design decisions visible in the launched experience. It does not claim research findings, analytics, or performance results that were not provided.

Research

Build confidence in layers, then offer the right next step

I structured the experience around four questions a homeowner needs answered before contacting a specialist.

1

What does it do for me?

Lead with a warmer, more usable pool and freedom from a monthly gas bill, not technical specifications.

2

How does it work?

Explain the loop simply: the existing pump moves water to roof panels, the sun warms it, and heated water returns to the pool.

3

Can I trust this company?

Bring 40+ years of experience, licensing, warranty coverage, real installations, and attributed reviews into the decision path.

4

What should I do next?

Offer a phone call, a quick satellite-based estimate, an advanced roof layout, and a standard request form for different comfort levels.

Solution

Five decisions shaped the final customer experience

01

Sell the outcome before the mechanism

"Actually enjoy your pool. Without the gas bill." frames the offer in the homeowner's language before introducing UV-stable mats or system mechanics.

02

Turn the service catalog into recognition

Installation, repair, replacement, removal, and inspection each receive a dedicated path. Supporting copy maps visible symptoms and life events to the appropriate service.

03

Make proof specific and verifiable

The experience combines the C-46 license number, 12-year panel warranty, decades in business, before-and-after work, certifications, and reviews linked to their source.

04

Let customers choose their quote commitment

The quote page distinguishes a direct call for a firm quote from a quick estimate, an advanced system designer, and a standard request form.

05

Design a content system, not a single page

Service pages, regional coverage, the solar explainer, gallery, FAQ, warranty, reviews, and live system data answer different objections without overloading the homepage.

Reflection

What this project demonstrates

This project required more than giving a local service company a polished interface. The work translated a technical category into a consumer decision journey, built an information architecture for several service intents, and connected education and trust to multiple conversion paths.

"Strong B2C design makes a complex purchase feel understandable without making the expertise behind it feel small."

What I would measure next

  • Which homepage paths lead to qualified quote requests
  • Where new buyers still need explanation before acting
  • How people choose between call, quick estimate, and advanced configuration
  • Which proof signals matter most by service type and device
